## Design specificity and strengths

The timecode faceplate, explicit transport, etched timeline and restrained phosphor/amber colors form a specific show-control instrument. The separate running-order workspace makes arrangement clearer, while the selected-entry inspector exposes the source timeline. Live gives the current entry and GO a strong hierarchy and states what GO does. Selection and operational states also use text, icons and strike-through rather than relying only on color.

## Priority issues

### P1: Prepared Live uses project-timeline navigation instead of the running order

Live renders TimelineStrip from project.timelines. It can show a timeline whose setlist entry is omitted and cannot represent repeated entries, aliases or setlist ordering. The true entry selector is inside the secondary disclosure. An operator has competing models of their position in the show.

Evidence: src/live/Live.tsx:32; src/components/TimelineStrip.tsx:85; src/show/ShowRun.tsx:35. Source and supplied screenshot agree; native tab switching was not exercised.

Fix: During a prepared show, prioritize a backend-provided running order with entry number/name, current/next state and omissions. Keep deliberate entry selection stopped-only. Suggested workflow: impeccable shape, then impeccable layout.

### P2: Running-order rows hide meaningful progression details

Rows give broad transition types but omit the exact configured delay and whether the timeline contains manual holds or an indefinite loop. Auditing the show requires selecting entries and opening their timelines.

Evidence: src/show/SetlistEditor.tsx:75 and :116.

Fix: Add concise backend-derived secondary summaries for exact delay, holds/loops and the source timeline when aliased. Keep titles dominant. Suggested workflow: impeccable clarify.

### P2: Loop range and execution context are too understated

The selected loop's full range has a faint boundary. The control list shows names/icons without time ranges or repeat mode. Add-at-playhead and prepared-setlist execution guidance is only shown when the control list is empty. Users may expect ordinary Edit playback to exercise these controls.

Evidence: src/show/ControlLayer.tsx:13; src/show/ControlEditor.tsx:27; src/styles.css:270-275. Screenshot supports the legibility concern; no rendered contrast measurement was possible.

Fix: Make the selected start/end boundaries readable, add secondary range/repeat summaries, and keep a compact execution-context hint near Add. Suggested workflow: impeccable clarify and impeccable layout.

### P2: Reordering controls are far from the running-order selection

Move up/down appear after notes and inclusion near the inspector bottom, sometimes requiring scrolling. Each position requires another click across the workspace.

Evidence: src/show/SetlistEditor.tsx:119; src/styles.css:243; supplied Setlists screenshot.

Fix: Put move actions near the selected row or in a sticky entry toolbar. Add documented keyboard movement and consider Move to for longer shows. Drag-and-drop is optional, not a prerequisite. Suggested workflow: impeccable layout, followed by impeccable polish.

## Cognitive load and emotional journey

Creation and entry editing are sensibly disclosed. The timeline nevertheless presents navigation, time mode, tempo/meter, click, snap, follow and view groups at once. Live requires relating the current/next entry, prepared/running cue and project timeline tabs; their relationship is the main remaining cognitive burden.

The running order provides a reassuring start and the dominant GO provides a clear operational focal point. Confidence drops when auditing loop behavior or locating the show among timeline tabs. Completion, fault recovery and real show transitions were not observed.

## Persona checks

- Experienced technician: direct GO and existing keyboard behavior are useful; one-step reordering and opening timelines to audit controls slow preparation.
- First-time FLUX operator with show experience: transition explanations help; entry/timeline identity and prepared-show-only controls need clearer context.
- Keyboard/low-vision operator: real buttons, explicit labels and status text are positive source evidence. Faint ranges and small secondary labels warrant rendered testing. Screen reader, keyboard end-to-end and contrast claims are not made.
